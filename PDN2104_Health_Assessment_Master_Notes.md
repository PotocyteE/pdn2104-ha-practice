## L3: Nutritional & Anthropometric Assessment

### BMI (WHO Standard)
**Formula:**  
BMI = Weight (kg) / [Height (m)]²

| BMI (kg/m²) | Classification (WHO) |
|-------------|----------------------|
| < 18.5 | Underweight |
| 18.5 – 24.9 | Normal weight |
| 25 – 29.9 | Overweight |
| 30 – 39.9 | Obesity |
| ≥ 40 | Extreme obesity |

**Note:** Not accurate for clients with oedema or highly athletic builds.  
**Chinese terms:** 身體質量指數 (BMI), 體重過輕, 正常, 超重, 肥胖, 極度肥胖

### Waist-to-Hip Ratio (WHR)
WHR = Waist circumference / Hip circumference  
- Waist: just above iliac crests  
- Hip: largest circumference of buttocks  

**Risk:**  
- Men ≥ 1.0 or Women ≥ 0.8 → Android (apple-shaped) obesity → higher cardiovascular risk  
- Gynoid (pear-shaped): fat mainly in hips/thighs

## L4_1: Thyroid Gland – Inspection & Palpation (Posterior & Anterior Approaches)

**Preparation:**  
- Position standing lamp to shine **tangentially** across the neck (highlights swelling).  
- Tilt head back slightly to stretch skin.  
- Inspect as client **swallows** (thyroid tissue moves up then settles). Give sip of water if needed.

### Posterior Approach (preferred)
1. Stand **behind** the client.  
2. Ask client to sit straight, then bend head slightly **forward and to the right** (relaxes right-side muscles).  
3. Use **left hand** fingers to push trachea slightly to the right.  
4. Curve **right fingers** between trachea and sternomastoid muscle, retract slightly.  
5. Ask client to take a sip of water / swallow.  
6. Feel thyroid move up under fingers.  
7. Reverse for left side.  

**Normal findings:**  
- Often **not palpable** in normal adult.  
- If felt (long thin neck): isthmus over tracheal rings; lateral lobes rubbery but smooth.  

**Abnormal:** Enlarged lobes easily palpated before swallowing, tender, nodules or lumps (e.g. Graves disease / hyperthyroidism).  

### Anterior Approach
1. Stand facing client. Identify isthmus with thumb **3 cm below thyroid cartilage** while client swallows.  
2. Tip head forward and to the right.  
3. Use right thumb to displace trachea slightly right.  
4. Hook left thumb and fingers around sternomastoid; feel for lobe enlargement on swallow.  

**If enlarged:** Auscultate for **bruit** (soft, pulsatile, whooshing sound with bell of stethoscope).  
- Normal: no bruit.  
- Bruit = turbulent flow → hyperplasia (hyperthyroidism).  

**Chinese terms:** 甲狀腺 (thyroid), 觸診 (palpation), 吞嚥 (swallow), 甲狀腺雜音 (thyroid bruit), 甲狀腺腫 (goitre)

## L4_2: Confrontation Test (Visual Fields)

**Purpose:** Screen for loss of **peripheral vision**. Compares client’s field with examiner’s (assume examiner’s is normal).

**Steps:**
1. Position yourself at **eye level, about 2 feet** away, looking straight at client.  
2. Client covers one eye with opaque card; you cover your **opposite** eye.  
3. You are testing the client’s **uncovered** eye.  
4. Hold a **wiggling finger** as target midline between you and client.  
5. Slowly advance finger **from the periphery** in several directions.  
6. Client says “now” when target is first seen – this should be the same moment you see it.  
7. For temporal direction, start finger somewhat **behind** the person.  
8. Estimate angle:  
   - Superior: ~50°  
   - Temporal: ~90°  
   - Inferior: ~70°  
   - Nasal: ~60°  

**Abnormal:** Client unable to see object when examiner can → peripheral field loss (glaucoma screening in older adults; also retinal disease, stroke).  

**Chinese terms:** 對視試驗 / 視野對比試驗 (confrontation test), 周邊視野 (peripheral vision), 青光眼 (glaucoma)

## L4_3: Whispered Voice Test

**Purpose:** Screen for high-frequency hearing loss.

**Steps:**
1. Stand **arm’s length (2 feet) behind** the client.  
2. Test **one ear at a time**. Mask the other ear: place one finger on tragus and push in/out of auditory meatus (prevents sound transmission around head).  
3. Move head to 1–2 feet from the ear being tested.  
4. **Exhale fully**, then whisper slowly a set of **3 random numbers and letters** (e.g. “5, B, 6”).  
5. If incorrect, repeat with a **different** combination of 3.  
6. Passing score: correct repetition of **4 out of possible 6** items.  
7. Test the other ear with a new set (e.g. “4, K, 2”).

**Normal:** Client repeats each correctly.  
**Abnormal:** Unable to repeat 3 of the 6 items → high-tone hearing loss.  

**Chinese terms:** 耳語試驗 (whispered voice test), 高頻聽力損失 (high-frequency hearing loss)

## L5: Respiratory System – Objective Data Collection

### Inspection
- **Chest configuration:** Symmetric, AP:transverse ratio 1:2 (adult).  
  Abnormal: Barrel chest (COPD), pectus excavatum/carinatum, scoliosis.  
- **Respiration quality/rate:** Relaxed posture, regular even rate (12–20/min adult).  
  Abnormal: Tripod position, nasal flaring, pursed-lip breathing, use of accessory muscles, cyanosis.  
- Skin colour consistent with genetic background; no cyanosis/pallor.

### Palpation
**Symmetric Chest Expansion (posterior & anterior):**  
1. Posterior: Place hands sideways on posterolateral chest wall, thumbs pointing toward spine at T9–T10.  
2. Ask client to take deep breath.  
3. Thumbs should move apart **symmetrically**.  

**Tactile (Vocal) Fremitus:**  
- Client says “ninety-nine” or “one-one-one” while you palpate with ulnar edge or palm.  
- Normal: Symmetric vibrations.  
- Increased: Consolidation (pneumonia).  
- Decreased: Obstruction, pleural effusion, emphysema, thick chest wall.

### Percussion
- Start at apices, compare side to side, intercostal spaces.  
- **Normal lung field:** Resonance (low-pitched, clear, hollow).  
- Abnormal: Dullness (consolidation, fluid), hyperresonance (emphysema, pneumothorax).

### Auscultation
**Normal breath sounds:**  
- Bronchial (trachea): high pitch, loud, inspiration < expiration.  
- Bronchovesicular (main bronchi): medium, inspiration = expiration.  
- Vesicular (peripheral): low pitch, soft, inspiration > expiration.  

**Adventitious sounds:**  
- Fine crackles: discontinuous high-pitched (pneumonia, heart failure).  
- Coarse crackles: low-pitched bubbling.  
- Wheeze (high/low): musical (asthma, obstruction).  
- Stridor: high-pitched inspiratory crowing (upper airway obstruction – emergency).  

**Voice sounds** (if abnormal findings): Bronchophony, egophony, whispered pectoriloquy.

**Chinese terms:** 視診 (inspection), 觸診 (palpation), 叩診 (percussion), 聽診 (auscultation), 對稱胸廓擴張 (symmetric chest expansion), 觸覺語顫 (tactile fremitus), 共鳴音 (resonance), 囉音 (crackles), 喘鳴 (wheeze), 喉鳴 (stridor)

## L6: Cardiovascular System (Key Techniques)

### Health History – Chest Pain (PQRST)
- **P**rovoking: What makes it worse/better?  
- **Q**uality: Sharp, dull, crushing, burning?  
- **R**egion/Radiation: Where? Does it radiate (arm, jaw, back)?  
- **S**everity: 0–10 scale.  
- **T**iming: Onset, duration, frequency, associated symptoms (dyspnoea, sweating, nausea).  

### Carotid Artery
**Palpation:**  
- Palpate **one side at a time**, medial to sternomastoid, gently.  
- Avoid excessive pressure on carotid sinus (can cause bradycardia, especially elderly).  
- Normal: Smooth contour, brisk upstroke, strength 2+, regular, bilateral equal.  

**Auscultation (for bruit):**  
- Use bell, client exhale and hold breath briefly.  
- Listen at angle of jaw, mid-cervical, base of neck.  
- Normal: No bruit. Bruit = turbulent flow (atherosclerosis).

### Jugular Venous Pressure (JVP)
1. Client at 30–45° elevation.  
2. Use **sternal angle (angle of Louis)** as reference.  
3. Vertical ruler on sternal angle; horizontal straight edge (T-square) aligned to highest level of venous pulsation.  
4. Read vertical distance.  
5. Document: e.g. “Internal jugular pulsations 3 cm above sternal angle at 30°.”  

**Normal:** < 3 cm above sternal angle.  
**Abnormal:** > 3 cm at 45° → heart failure, tamponade, constrictive pericarditis.  

### Abdominojugular Test (Hepatojugular reflux)
- Client supine at 30–45°. Observe jugular pulsation.  
- Firm pressure on RUQ (liver) for 30–60 seconds.  
- Positive if JVP rises and stays elevated → right heart failure.  

### Precordium Auscultation
Standard areas: Aortic (2nd right ICS), Pulmonic (2nd left ICS), Erb’s point (3rd left ICS), Tricuspid (lower left sternal border), Mitral (5th ICS midclavicular).  
Listen with diaphragm then bell; note rate, rhythm, S1/S2, extra sounds, murmurs.

**Chinese terms:** 頸動脈 (carotid), 頸靜脈壓 (JVP), 胸骨角 (sternal angle / angle of Louis), 雜音 (bruit/murmur), 腹部頸靜脈試驗 (abdominojugular test)

## L7: Abdomen – Key Special Tests

### Umbilicus – Cullen’s Sign
Bluish discolouration around umbilicus → intraperitoneal bleeding.

### Costovertebral Angle (CVA) Tenderness
- Client sitting. Place one hand over 12th rib at CVA, strike with ulnar surface of other fist.  
- Normal: No pain. Abnormal: Pain → kidney inflammation (pyelonephritis).

### Fluid Wave Test (Ascites)
1. Stand on client’s right side.  
2. Client places ulnar edge of hand firmly on abdomen in midline (stops transmission of skin wave).  
3. Place your left hand on client’s right flank.  
4. With right hand, strike client’s left flank firmly.  
5. **Positive:** Distinct fluid wave felt on left hand → ascites.  
6. Negative if only gas/adipose.

### Shifting Dullness
1. Client supine. Percuss from midline (tympany) toward flanks until dullness (mark).  
2. Turn client to right side. Percuss again – level of dullness should shift upward if fluid present.  
- Indicates free fluid in peritoneal cavity.  

### Murphy’s Sign (Liver/Gallbladder)
- Palpate under right costal margin while client takes deep breath.  
- **Positive Murphy’s:** Sudden inspiratory arrest due to pain → acute cholecystitis.

### Rebound Tenderness
- Press deeply and slowly into abdomen, then release quickly.  
- Normal: No pain on release.  
- Abnormal: Sharp pain on release → peritoneal inflammation (peritonitis).

**Chinese terms:** 卡倫氏徵 (Cullen’s sign), 肋脊角壓痛 (CVA tenderness), 波動試驗 (fluid wave), 移動性濁音 (shifting dullness), 墨菲氏徵 (Murphy’s sign), 反跳痛 (rebound tenderness), 腹水 (ascites)

## L9: Musculoskeletal – Key Special Tests

### Phalen Test (Carpal Tunnel)
- Hold both hands back-to-back with wrists flexed 90° for **60 seconds**.  
- Normal: No symptoms.  
- Positive: Numbness and burning → carpal tunnel syndrome (median nerve).

### Tinel Sign
- Direct percussion over median nerve at wrist.  
- Normal: No symptoms.  
- Positive: Burning and tingling → carpal tunnel.

### Knee Effusion Tests
**Bulge Sign** (small amount, 4–8 ml):  
1. Firmly stroke up medial aspect of knee 2–3 times (displace fluid).  
2. Tap lateral aspect and watch for fluid wave in medial hollow.  
- Positive wave = small effusion.

**Ballottement Test** (larger amount):  
1. Compress suprapatellar pouch with one hand.  
2. Push patella sharply against femur with other hand.  
- Positive: Patella floats / rebounds (fluid present).

### Straight Leg Raising (SLR / Lasègue Test)
- Client supine. Raise extended leg.  
- Positive: Pain radiating down leg between 30–70° → lumbar disc herniation / sciatica.

### Leg Length Discrepancy
- Measure from anterior superior iliac spine (ASIS) to medial malleolus bilaterally.  
- Difference > 1 cm significant.

**Chinese terms:** 法倫試驗 (Phalen test), 蒂內爾徵 (Tinel sign), 膨出徵 (bulge sign), 浮髌試驗 (ballottement), 直腿抬高試驗 (SLR / Lasègue), 腿長差異 (leg length discrepancy), 腕隧道症候群 (carpal tunnel syndrome)

## Quick Documentation Tips (Common in SQ)
Always document:  
- Technique performed  
- Findings (normal or specific abnormal)  
- Laterality  
- Client position when relevant (e.g. JVP at 30°)  

**Example (Thyroid):** “Thyroid not palpable. No nodules, no bruit. Moves symmetrically with swallowing.”  

**Example (JVP):** “Internal jugular vein pulsations 2 cm above sternal angle at 45° elevation. No abdominojugular reflux.”
